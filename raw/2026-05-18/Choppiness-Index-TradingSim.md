## Overview of the Choppiness Index Indicator

The choppiness index indicator is an oscillator. Like many other oscillators, the range for the choppiness index is 0 to 100. The choppiness index indicator uses a standard lookback period of 14 days and takes into account the average true range indicator, price high, and price low to determine a percentage value.

The upper and lower limits are set to the 61.8% and 38.2% retracement levels (Fibonacci). If the indicator is above 61.8%, then the stock is experiencing a choppy trend; however, if the reading is below 38.2% the stock is beginning to trend. Therefore, the closer you are to 100, the choppier the market and the closer to 0, the greater the trend.

## 4 Trading Strategies for how to use the Choppiness Indicator

### #1 – Buy or Sell the Breakout after extreme Choppiness Index Readings

When a stock is trending above the 61.8% reading for an extended period of time, this is a sign that the market is beyond flat but practically dead.

Instead of buying or selling the break of the 38.2% retracement, another approach is to wait for a fall back below the 61.8% retracement level to signal a trend is in its infancy.

Key points:
- The choppiness indicator would be best used for gauging a breakout after lunch (mid-day flat period)
- It is critical for this breakout strategy to (1) occur in the late afternoon and (2) have extreme readings on the choppy index for 1 to 2 hours on a 5-minute chart
- This is a sign that when a breakout occurs as the stock is starting to trend, you may be able to catch some late-day fire

### #2 – Ride the Trend using the Choppiness Index Indicator

If the choppiness indicator does not print 3 or more readings above the 61.8% retracement, and the stock is in a strong trend, hold on for the ride.

What is valuable about this approach is that you can weed out all of the false readings, as these pullbacks are just noise inside of the context of the primary trend.

The key point is that you have to develop a solid system for determining when a stock is starting to trend. If you are unable to consistently identify a trending stock, you will find yourself making trade decisions based on false signals.

### #3 Trade within Choppy Markets

The choppiness index indicator is not like an oversold or overbought indicator, so trying to time the moves inside of a tight range could prove a little difficult and may need a little help from a stochastics or Williams R.

Just because the indicator is at 61.8% does not mean the stock will all of a sudden start trending. You really need price action to increase the level of certainty provided by the indicator.

Trading the chop has not served well over the years. Choppiness may match some personalities but is generally not recommended.

### #4 – Walk away from stocks that do not trade nicely with the Choppiness Index Indicator

Some stocks will not adhere to the nice boundaries of 61.8% and 38.2% for the choppiness index indicator. There will be all sorts of false signals above and below the boundaries.

If you cannot make heads or tails of it, please do not start modifying the settings to fit each security perfectly. You want to use the fact the stock does not adhere to the boundaries as a reason to filter out the stock from your list of potential candidates.

## Summary

The choppiness index indicator does help identify when a stock is experiencing volatility; however, trading choppy markets is not where the indicator excels. The strength in the indicator is best displayed when used as a confirmation that a stock is starting to trend or breaking out.

A simple, yet effective way to validate signs from the choppiness index indicator is to see if volume accompanies the move. Where there is volume, there is likely something brewing.

If you find yourself having to customize any indicator, you are trying to fit a square peg into a round hole. In the same way you do not force trades, you should not force indicators to fit stocks or markets that trade differently.
